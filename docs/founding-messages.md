## 1. The life-protocol idea

> I'm trying to create an AI model to help me keep track of the state of a life protocol. And what that means is something similar to what this YouTuber Brian Johnson does, where he has a protocol of a set of practices or tools or products that he uses for whatever health goals he has. This includes the type of exercises or sports that he performs, the type of treatments that he gets for hair or nails, or for the skin. Can you go check his website? You might be able to fetch more information than I can provide.

## 2. The main drivers

> Yeah, so I guess that's what I want to start doing because I already have a set of things that I use. Some of them I may be locked in. Some others are just products that I chose just for picking something without a particular preference. And I was thinking, yes, I can keep a list of the products that I own and just be aware of the amounts that I have or the amounts that I use, but that's something that I know already, right? It's not something that usually you need assistance for. A lot of people just remember what face wash they use. When it's about run out, they just go buy a new one. Same for toothbrush. They just pick something from the store and it works. They already know the shampoo that they use. They have the bottle for reference as well. Like this is not something that I feel requires extremely demanding AI usage to keep track of, right? A lot of these things just are life. So my question was, I know the system may not need to be overly complex. In fact, I would like to avoid it being overly complex. I'd probably keep it as simple as possible as you could on this first iteration, and then maybe you could later improve on them. But I did want a basis, and the main driver that I have for the system is that oftentimes you run into tools that the world is recommending you. Like you see a short, it's talking about this magic shampoo or this new vitamin or new anything. And it's very exhausting to have to constantly be up to date with the latest nutrient, the latest product, what actually fits best with the other products you use, if it matters, or just know if it's worth it to change something, or the science has changed, or your necessities have changed, and you have to update whatever you're doing. But every time just analyzing, trying to memorize what are the good products to look for, like what vitamins, or filtering down products manually, like all of these things are exhaustive, right? And being up to the state of the art as well is just not something that's easy to do for most people without dedicating significant time. So what I was hoping was that this AI system would automate this part, right? Like be able to receive the noise: new products, new ads, new whatever, and just try to build something that makes sense for me. And normally that then comes a part of matching to logistics, because there must be maybe perfect products, but maybe they are out of the budget range at the moment, and the extra benefit is not worth it compared to another product that is a little cheaper, way cheaper, might be a little less efficient, but it works for me, right? Or mapping things to reality, like you could have like a thousand of different items, but maybe your store only sells ten of them. So that also is exhaustive. Imagine making such an investigation and then finding out that what you want is not even available. So these are the main drivers that I have behind the idea of looking for AI to help me automate the protocol.

## 4. Synchronization and readability concerns

> I just have two observations. The first one is that I feel it's easy for things to drift out of reality unless there is a sort of synchronization between what I'm actually doing. Like, how is the system supposed to know? I don't know if it's relevant, by the way, but I'm just saying, like, how does it know if I'm using something, if I've stopped using something, if I don't regularly check in? Like, we gotta... I'm not saying that the agent should interview me every day, but I'm saying, how are we keeping ourselves safe from running into situations where the agent, just because I told it I was using a shampoo once, assumes that I'm using it still, and I talk to it six months later and it assumes that I've been using it for six months. So that's one thing. The other one is that the other observation I have of the system is that the protocol that you have is pretty much a single table database. I see that you have a sort of Markdown table there, which is just a record of activities or whatever it is. But I don't know how readable or easy to navigate is that if we compare it to Brian Johnson's, which is grouped by category, or exactly by that, and also is very easy to read and go through. Whereas I feel yours maybe is optimizing for something else, but I imagine it can be hard to read if you get like 100 records on this table of yours. I'm not saying it's wrong or that the system is not going to work. I'm just concerned about how it scales up in terms of readability.
