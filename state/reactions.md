## Recording a reaction

Keep one section per reported product or practice, identified by its name and linked practice ID when one exists. A reaction can be recorded even if it has no practice card. Include:

- Report date and source; reaction/event date if separately provided.
- The user's description of the reaction and its course, with uncertainty intact.
- Product variant/formulation, relevant ingredients, amount, frequency, body site, and other use context as known; leave missing details unknown.
- What the user did afterward and any reported outcome, including stopping, restarting, or a clinician's assessment when supplied.
- Whether a cause is confirmed, suspected by the user, or unknown. A reaction while using a product does not by itself identify a responsible ingredient or establish an allergy.

Keep material follow-up reports and corrections in the same product section with their actual report dates. Preserve the original context instead of transferring a reaction to a replacement product. Read related entries even when the cause is unknown; ingredient-name matching alone is insufficient. Link supporting research only for its assessed outcome and context. Do not infer an ingredient or personal reaction from a hypothetical example.
