# Review a claim or possible change

1. Identify the question, objective/outcome, use context, constraints, and prior research. Read the overview, relevant category cards, and applicable topic/outcome sections in `research/`. Search product names, relevant ingredients or synonyms, and the claim/outcome across `research/` and `state/` for related context, including candidate reviews and previous setups that are not linked from the latest card. Read matching sections and follow correction/reassessment notices; do not load all history by default. Include related categories when compatibility or overlap matters. Historical or Needs reassessment links do not support the current setup. Reuse research only when its objective, context, and revisit conditions still apply; an explicit reassessment requires a new evidence check.
2. Before a personalized product comparison or recommendation, read `state/reactions.md` even if no adverse experience is remembered. Consider relevant prior product reports including stopped products and unknown causes, not just exact ingredient matches. Verify proposed and historical formulation details when material; a current label does not establish what was in a past product. A shared ingredient is a reason to assess relevance, not proof of causation or allergy. If nothing relevant is found, distinguish “no relevant report found in recorded history” from “no reaction ever occurred.” A hypothetical evidence question does not authorize importing the user's history into that hypothetical person.
3. Determine whether the claim addresses a relevant need. An absent baseline is unknown, not evidence of need. Check dated status and frequency/amount, and selectively reconfirm older reports when the recommendation depends on current use or exposure. Months-old or contradictory context may warrant a focused question. If unanswered, keep dependent conclusions conditional and continue independent research. Do not infer continuous use or require a full routine audit.
4. Check plausibility and practical access early. Before a long comparison, establish shopping region and approximate budget when material, and whether viable options exist. Preliminary evidence research is possible without those facts; purchasing conclusions remain unresolved.
5. For substantive health recommendations, examine relevant clinical guidance, high-quality systematic reviews/meta-analyses, and primary studies as appropriate. Check quality, relevance, search currency, and underlying/newer primary work when material. Scope evidence to outcome, population, formulation, dose, route, duration, and meaningful endpoints. Distinguish ingredient/mechanism evidence from marketed-product outcomes. Do not transfer evidence across purposes or regimens without labeling the inference and limits. Note uncertainty, relevant harms, and conflicts of interest.
6. Compare with the reported setup: overlap, compatibility, personal experience, switching effort, total cost, ongoing burden, and incremental benefit. Verify material formulation, retailer, price/currency, shipping, and stock/access dates. Do not equate a global listing with local availability. Stop when more research is unlikely to change the practical conclusion; resolve consequential uncertainty or leave the conclusion conditional instead of surveying the whole market.
7. Create or update the relevant topic page using `.agents/templates/research.md` and `research/README.md`. Give each assessed objective a clear conclusion, use context, review date, reasoning, sources/access dates, and revisit condition. Reuse topic/outcome sections; do not create a dated file per review. Keep the research index's links current and replace its empty-state note when the first actual topic is created. Preserve prior material conclusions and context in the same page's labeled revisions when they change. An unchanged recheck needs only a concise dated explanation of what was checked.
8. For an existing practice, link the applicable research outcome section under Evidence by objective on its card; update only the assessed objective/context and applicability label. Preserve unrelated research, personal observations/history, all reported-use fields and dates, and reactions entries. Candidate-only research does not create a practice. Respond with the conclusion, reason relative to the baseline, practical options when warranted, uncertainty, and saved link. If the user also adopts a change, follow the update workflow.

An advertisement, ownership, or the assistant's enthusiasm is not adoption. A negative or uncertain finding can prevent repeated investigation. Research review dates do not confirm use, and revisit conditions do not authorize scheduled work.

## Check the reasoning before concluding

Apply this within the evidence review, before comparison and recommendation; it is not a separate exhaustive research phase.

- Identify the premise or missing causal step whose failure would change the conclusion. Check what the cited work actually measured, in whom, under what conditions and for how long. Distinguish direct evidence for the target outcome from a mechanism, surrogate, or extrapolation. Improvement in a reversible marker does not by itself establish restoration of lost structure, absence of cumulative damage, or a long-term clinical benefit.
- Seek relevant evidence against the consequential premise as well as for it. Resolve material conflicts by examining methods, endpoints and applicability, not by counting citations. Verify scientific claims independently even when supplied as a correction. If a challenge is unsupported, explain why rather than agreeing reflexively.
- Match the wording to the evidence: reduced risk, no detected effect, and demonstrated absence of an effect are different claims. Do not derive a universal safe threshold from average effects or laboratory results. Equally, uncertainty about zero risk does not establish harm on every exposure.
- Recommend when the balance of evidence and practical stakes warrants it. Explain the uncertainty that could change the action; do not require certainty about every mechanism. If a key premise cannot be checked, qualify or withhold the dependent claim and continue any recommendation supported independently. Record only the material premise, evidence boundary and resulting implication, not an exhaustive internal deliberation.

## Correct a material premise and its consequences

Use this whenever a material error is found during requested work.

1. Identify the precise claim, outcome/context, and source of error; verify the proposed replacement independently. Distinguish established error from a concern requiring verification.
2. Search `research/` and `state/` for the affected topic path/heading and claim text. Read matching pages and follow dependent reasoning until no material dependencies remain. Search text as well as links to find uncited reuse; this is a scoped dependency check, not a whole-protocol audit.
3. Correct the current outcome section on its topic page, keeping its prior statement, date, relevant reasoning, sources, and context in a clearly labeled revision with a dated notice and link to the current conclusion. Explain the exact corrected scope; preserve unrelated conclusions and original statements as history.
4. On each affected topic page, label the prior affected conclusion superseded for its scope or Needs reassessment. Record whether dependent advice changed, remains justified independently, or needs reassessment, with reasons. Do not automatically reverse every recommendation when one premise fails. Refresh affected card research links; unresolved dependencies must not support a recommendation. State what remains unchecked if evidence/access limits the correction.
5. Keep actual use, frequency/amount, confirmation dates, personal history, and canonical reactions unchanged unless a user report changes them. Report which advice changed and the practical consequence. Corrected evidence does not imply stopping or adoption. A newer page/review date alone does not establish broader scope.

## Example of older state

A shampoo was last confirmed active six months ago. If asked whether it failed to improve hair, the dates do not establish six months of exposure. Clarify relevant use, frequency, and gaps when duration matters, while continuing general evidence research. Do not convert elapsed time into adherence.

## Reassess selected knowledge on request

For “update knowledge” or a challenge, identify the practice, outcome, or topic. If unspecified, ask which area matters rather than reviewing everything. Read earlier reasoning and notices, verify updated evidence and material premises, and reassess formulation, needs, and practical fit. Update the same topic/outcome section with the review date and what changed or was retained; preserve materially changed prior reasoning in labeled revisions. Supersede only the outcome/context actually rechecked. Update the matching card research link without confirming use. This is on-demand review, not monitoring.
