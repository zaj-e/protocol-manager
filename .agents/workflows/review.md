# Review a claim or possible change

1. Identify the actual question, objective/outcome being assessed, relevant use context, constraints, and prior decision. Read the overview, relevant category cards, and any applicable objective-specific record. Search `decisions/` by topic/outcome for standalone findings too; follow correction and supersession notices before reusing a record. Include related categories when compatibility or overlap requires them. Check the last status confirmation and dated frequency/amount, not just the status label. Inspect any replacement history and evidence applicability labels: Historical or Needs reassessment links do not establish support for the current setup. For repeated claims, reuse a conclusion only when its objective, use context, and reconsideration conditions still apply. An explicit request to reassess triggers a new evidence check even if the old record exists.
2. Determine whether the claim addresses a relevant need. An absent baseline is unknown, not evidence that the user needs the advertised product. Before an older report materially affects the recommendation, ask a focused question about the relevant current use and any important dose/frequency. Months-old reports or contradictory context warrant reconfirmation when the conclusion depends on them. If the user does not answer, keep dependent conclusions conditional and continue general research where possible. Do not ask for a full routine audit or infer continuous use from elapsed time.
3. Check basic plausibility and practical access early. Before a long product comparison, establish the shopping region, approximate budget when important, and whether viable options exist. If these are unknown, a preliminary evidence review is still possible; label purchasing conclusions unresolved.
4. For substantive health recommendations, examine relevant clinical guidance, high-quality systematic reviews/meta-analyses, and primary studies as appropriate. Check quality, relevance, and how current the underlying search is; examine underlying or newer studies when material. Scope evidence to the specific objective/outcome, population, formulation, dose, route/use, and duration. Distinguish an ingredient or mechanism from evidence for the marketed product and meaningful outcomes. Do not transfer evidence across purposes, formulations, or regimens without labeling the inference and its limits. Note important uncertainty, relevant harms, and conflicts of interest.
5. Compare with what the user already uses or does. Consider overlap, compatibility, reported experience, switching effort, total cost, ongoing burden, and the size of the expected incremental benefit. For practical product suggestions, verify formulation, retailer, price/currency, shipping where material, and stock/access dates. Do not call a global listing locally available without checking.
6. Stop when more research is unlikely to change the practical conclusion. Do not pursue a comprehensive market survey or invented numerical ranking. Resolve material uncertainty or leave the conclusion explicitly conditional.
7. Save a concise decision using `decisions/template.md`, with an explicit objective/outcome and use context. A review's conclusion is keep current setup, consider change, or unresolved. For a standalone knowledge question, use a qualified finding instead of forcing an action verdict. Related items may be none; use a descriptive topic/outcome title so the finding can be found in `decisions/` without a practice card. Separate conclusions if multiple outcomes are assessed. State the condition for reconsideration. For an existing practice, link the review under Evidence by objective on its card; update only the assessed objective/context, resolving its applicability label when justified and preserving unrelated reviews and all reported-use fields. Candidate-only and standalone reviews stay in decisions. Recording a review does not establish adoption or refresh status confirmation.
8. Respond with the conclusion, the main reason relative to the baseline, practical options only if warranted, and any material uncertainty. Link the saved record. If the user also explicitly adopts a change, continue with the update workflow.

An advertisement, ownership, or the assistant's enthusiasm is never evidence of adoption. A negative or uncertain review is still worth remembering if it prevents repeated investigation.

## Check the reasoning before concluding

Apply this within the evidence review, before comparison and recommendation; it is not a separate exhaustive research phase.

- Identify the premise or missing causal step whose failure would change the conclusion. Check what the cited work actually measured, in whom, under what conditions and for how long. Distinguish direct evidence for the target outcome from a mechanism, surrogate, or extrapolation. Improvement in a reversible marker does not by itself establish restoration of lost structure, absence of cumulative damage, or a long-term clinical benefit.
- Seek relevant evidence against the consequential premise as well as for it. Resolve material conflicts by examining methods, endpoints and applicability, not by counting citations. Verify scientific claims independently even when supplied as a correction. If a challenge is unsupported, explain why rather than agreeing reflexively.
- Match the wording to the evidence: reduced risk, no detected effect, and demonstrated absence of an effect are different claims. Do not derive a universal safe threshold from average effects or laboratory results. Equally, uncertainty about zero risk does not establish harm on every exposure.
- Recommend when the balance of evidence and practical stakes warrants it. Explain the uncertainty that could change the action; do not require certainty about every mechanism. If a key premise cannot be checked, qualify or withhold the dependent claim and continue any recommendation supported independently. Record only the material premise, evidence boundary and resulting implication, not an exhaustive internal deliberation.

## Correct a material premise and its consequences

Use this whenever an error is found during a requested task, not only when the user explicitly asks for correction.

1. Identify the precise claim, objective/context and source of the error; check the proposed replacement against evidence. Distinguish a demonstrated error from a concern still needing verification.
2. Search `decisions/` and `state/` for the old record ID/path and the affected claim/topic, then read the matching records. Follow dependent reasoning through further records until no material dependencies remain. Search text as well as links: a conclusion may have been reused without a citation. This is a scoped dependency check, not a full protocol audit.
3. Add a dated review with the corrected or qualified finding. For each affected conclusion, say whether it changes, remains justified on independent evidence, or needs reassessment. State the reason and link the affected records. Do not automatically reverse every recommendation when one premise fails.
4. Add a prominent dated notice to each affected old record linking the new review and identifying the exact superseded scope, or marking it Needs reassessment if unresolved. Preserve its original text and unaffected conclusions. Refresh affected card evidence links; unresolved dependencies must be visibly Needs reassessment and must not support a recommendation. Record what remains unchecked if access or evidence blocks completion.
5. Keep reported use, dose/frequency, confirmation dates and state-decision links unchanged unless a new user report changes them. Tell the user which advice changed and any practical consequence. An evidence correction does not mean a practice was stopped. Before reuse, read correction notices and follow their links; a newer date alone does not establish broader supersession.

## Example of older state

A shampoo card was last confirmed active six months ago. The user asks whether it failed to improve their hair. Say that the last report does not establish six months of use. Ask whether they still use it, how often, and whether there were gaps if treatment duration matters. Do not evaluate six months of treatment merely from the old confirmation date. A general explanation of the evidence can proceed while use history remains unknown.

## Reassess selected knowledge on request

For “update knowledge” or “challenge this conclusion,” identify the practice, objective, or category to reassess. If the scope is unspecified, ask which area matters rather than launch a full-protocol audit. Read the earlier reasoning, search for relevant updated evidence, and reconsider applicability, formulation, needs, and practical fit. Record whether the conclusion changed and why; a fresh review can legitimately retain the same recommendation. Add a new dated record linking the earlier one and mark supersession only for the assessed objective/context. Refresh the card's evidence link and review date, never its use confirmation. This is an on-demand review, not a scheduled monitoring service.
